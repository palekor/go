Station 1 · Space

Point from your body

Deictic words point. Their meaning changes with your position: your body is the center of the map. Near you is here. Far from you is there.

The 2 × 2 map

Spanish has three distances. English has two.

ESesteeseaquel ENthisthat

Ese and aquel both become that. Our rule from class: if it is within reach of your fingers, it is this. If you need to point with your arm, it is that.

Hear the difference: this · these

${SAY:This book.}this /ðɪs/ short i, soft s
${SAY:These books.}these /ðiːz/ long ee, buzzing z like a bee

Tip: if you say “these” short, the listener hears “this”. Stretch the vowel.

that has a second job: que

In our notes: that = eso, (que). That is correct. that also connects a noun with more information.

${SAY:The house that I love is in Armenia.}The house that I love is in Armenia. La casa que amo…
${SAY:My mother thinks that this house is beautiful.}My mother thinks that this house is beautiful.

Place words and time words

here aquí · acá   there allí · allá · ahí

${SAY:Come here! Sit with me.}Come here! Sit with me.
${SAY:Armenia is beautiful. I want to live there.}Armenia is beautiful. I want to live there.

Deixis also works in time. Now is near: this week, this morning. A story in the past is far: that day, that night.

      Station 2 · Existence

      Declare what exists: THERE( )

      Spanish uses one small word: hay. English builds a small machine: There + is / are. Think of it as a function. You put a noun inside, and the noun decides the verb.

      One word in Spanish, one machine in English

      THERE( noun ) {
  // one thing, or something you cannot count
  There is  a big dog · some water
  // two or more things
  There are three white cats
}

      Haber = hay. There is for singular, There are for plural. Uncountable nouns (water, music, information) always take is.

      Three forms

      One / uncountableTwo or more
      +There is a bathroom.There’s a bathroom.There are four bedrooms.
      −There isn’t a garage.There is no garage.There aren’t any cats.There are no cats.
      ?Is there a bathroom?Yes, there is. / No, there isn’t.Are there any cats?Yes, there are. / No, there aren’t.

      Four traps for Spanish speakers

      • Have three cats in my house.There are three cats in my house.hay is not have. have = tener (I have three cats). There are = existir.
      • There is three cats.There are three cats.The noun decides. Three cats = plural → are.
      • Is a dog in the park.There is a dog in the park.Spanish can drop the subject. English never does. There is the subject.
      • There house is big.Their house is big.Same sound /ðer/: there (place, hay) · their (su, de ellos) · they’re (they are).

      Two theres in one sentence

      ${SAY:There is a café there.}There is a café there. Hay un café allá.

      The first there declares existence. The second there is a place. This is where Station 1 and Station 2 meet.

      The THERE( ) builder

      Change the number, the noun and the form. The machine writes the sentence.

            Station 3 · Time lens

            Choose your time lens

            We learn English and We are learning English. Both are correct. They show two different pictures of time, and you choose the picture.

            Simple lens

            I learn English.

            I · you · we · they learn  |  he · she · it learns

            Routines, habits and facts. It happens again and again: every Wednesday, always, often, sometimes.

            ${SAY:I cook dinner with my family because I like it.}I cook dinner with my family because I like it.

            aprendo · cocino

            Progressive lens

            I am learning English.

            am · is · are  +  verb-ing  (two components)

            An action in progress: now, right now, this week, this month. It started and it is not finished.

            ${SAY:We are learning connectors this week.}We are learning connectors this week.

            estoy aprendiendo · estamos aprendiendo

            State verbs stay simple

            Some verbs describe a state, not an action. We usually do not use them with -ing.

            likelovehatewantneedknowbelievehave = tenerthink = creer

            They are liking this house.
            They like this house.

            From our notes. In Spanish “les está gustando” is fine. In English, like is a state, so it stays simple.

            have or has?

            haveI · you · we · they
            hashe · she · it · my mother · this house · Armenia
            ${SAY:They like this house because it has three bathrooms.}They like this house because it has three bathrooms.

            The same -s appears in the simple lens: my mother thinks, she learns, it makes me happy.

                Station 4 · Logic

                Connect ideas with logic

                Short sentences in a row sound like a list. Connectors show the relation between two ideas: addition, contrast, cause, result, time. These are the fourteen super connectors from our chart, organized by the job they do.

                The connector atlas

                Tap a connector to see its job, its Spanish and an example.

                because ↔ so

                One idea, two directions. because introduces the cause. so introduces the result.

                Small fixes to our printed chart

                • because = porque (one word)por qué with a space is a question: why.
                • often = a menudo · con frecuenciaseguido is informal Spanish. It works in conversation.
                • then = luego · después   so = así que · entoncesthen orders time. so gives a result.
                • about = sobre · acerca deI think about my future = pienso en mi futuro. Never “think in”.
                • for = para · por · durantefor my family (para) · for two hours (durante).

                Where does also go?

                Before the main verb, or after am / is / are.

                and also I think about my future
                and I also think about my future

                Spanish says “y también pienso”. English moves also next to the verb.

                Spanish y can hide a but

                En esta casa hay 4 cuartos y 1 solo baño. The two ideas are in contrast, so English needs but:

                ${SAY:There are four bedrooms in this house, but there is only one bathroom.}There are four bedrooms in this house, but there is only one bathroom.

                  Station 5 · Precision

                  Paint the noun

                  Adjectives give color and precision to your descriptions. In English they are simpler than in Spanish: one position, one form.

                  Before the noun

                  a house big → a big house una casa grande

                  After be, the adjective goes alone: The house is big.

                  Never plural

                  ${SAY:There are three white cats in my house.}There are three white cats. whites cats

                  Spanish: gatos blancos. English: only the noun takes -s.

                  a or an?

                  The next sound decides: a big dog · an interesting book · an old house.

                  Premium order: opinion before facts

                  ${SAY:It is a beautiful big white house.}a beautiful (opinion) big (size) white (color) house
                  Fixes to our printed chart
                  • fun = divertido (a party, a game). funny = gracioso, chistoso (it makes you laugh).
                  • acid is for chemistry. For taste we say sour: Lemons are sour.
                  • educative is rare. Natural English: educational.
