1

The engine

Three rules that never change

Modals are the easiest verbs in English: they never change form. One form works for every person. Learn these three rules and every sentence in this class works the same way.

Rule 1

Modal + base verb

After a modal, the verb stays in its base form. No to, no -s, no -ing.

She can speak English.
She cans speak · She can to speak
Rule 2

Question: the modal jumps

Move the modal before the subject. Never use do or does with a modal.

Can she speak English?
Does she can speak English?
Rule 3

Negative: modal + not

Add not after the modal. Most forms contract.

She can't speak French.
She doesn't can speak French.

3

Ask with authority

Questions with modals

There are only two formulas. The first one asks for a yes or a no. The second one adds a WH word at the start to ask for information.

Formula A · Yes / No
Modal+Subject+Base verb?
Could you send me the report?
Should we wait for Maria?
Will they arrive on time?
Formula B · Information
WH+Modal+Subject+Base verb?

Short answers: repeat the modal

QuestionYesNo
No contraction in a yes. Say Yes, I will. Never Yes, I'll. In a no, the contraction is normal: No, I won't.

Permission: ask, give and refuse

Ask · informal to formal
Give permission
Refuse politely
The “Do you mind…?” trap. The question means “is it a problem for you if…?”. To say yes to the person, you answer with a no: Not at all. Go ahead. If you answer Yes, you are saying it is a problem.

Train your ear

The Sound Lab

Modals are almost never pronounced in full. If you wait for the dictionary word, you will miss it. Listen to how they sound in real conversation.

Can · Can't

Positive can is weak and fast: /kən/. Negative can't is strong and long: /kænt/. Listen for the stress, not the T.

The silent L

Could, should and would rhyme with good. The L is never pronounced.

Words that link together
